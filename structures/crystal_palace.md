# Palacio de Cristal, Retiro, Madrid: reference dimensions

Architect Ricardo Velázquez Bosco. Structural design and calculations by Alberto de Palacio Elissague. Ironwork erected by Bernardo Asins; iron cast by Alonso, Millán y Cía, Bilbao. Ceramics by Daniel (and Germán) Zuloaga. Built in less than five months in 1887 for the Exposición General de las Islas Filipinas. [1][3][4][5]

## Summary

The building is a glass-and-iron "pabellón-estufa" (hothouse) on a stone and brick plinth. Its plan is a Greek cross whose front arm is replaced by a stone Ionic portico. The other three arms each have a high central nave and a lower aisle that wraps round the nave's end, like a Gothic chevet with an ambulatory. The two side arms are longer than the back arm. Over the crossing is a glazed four-panel cloister vault (*cúpula esquifada*). The naves have glazed barrel roofs. The aisles have curved lean-to roofs.

The published "54 × 28 m" and "22.60 m" figures go back to a few repeated sources. Cadastral cartography, OpenStreetMap and the PNOA orthophoto all measure the footprint as about **56 × 34 m at the roof edge**, or **38 m deep including the portico block**. The 28 m width is not supported. The dome-height conflict (22.60 m against 24 m) is probably a difference of datum: interior floor against exterior ground. See [Overall dimensions](#overall-dimensions).

Confidence labels used in this document:

- **confirmed**: two or more independent sources agree.
- **single**: only one source, or several sources that copy one origin.
- **estimated**: my own measurement. The method is given in [Methods](#methods-for-the-estimates).

Unless stated otherwise, heights are measured from the exterior ground at the foot of the plinth.

## Overall dimensions

### Orientation and position

- The long axis bears about 346°/166° (N 14° W).
- The portico faces about **076° (ENE)**, toward the lake.
- The crossing centre is at about E 442136.5, N 4473886.9 (ETRS89 / UTM 30N), from Catastro [9].

### Length (54 m against 56 m)

- The only published figure, 54 m, appears in [1][2][15] and in many blogs. These sources do not look independent: they repeat the same sentence.
- Catastro, OSM and the orthophoto independently give 56.0–56.3 m to the outer roof edge.
- The 1975 plan's wall line and its outer line differ by about 4%: 458 px against 476 px.
- **Conclusion:** use **≈ 54 m wall-to-wall** (the published figure) and **≈ 56 m at the eaves** (measured). This implies a gutter and cornice overhang of about 1 m each side. The side-arm widths support this: 17.8 m at the walls in the plan against 19.7 m at the eaves.

### Width / depth (28 m against 32 m)

- Every measured front-to-back figure is larger than 28 m:
  - 31.4 m at the wall line (plan)
  - 34.0 m at the roof edge (Catastro, OSM; orthophoto 33.5)
  - 38.0 m including the portico
- Your ~32 m pixel estimate is consistent with the wall-line value.
- The 28 m may be a garbled figure. It equals the crossing-centre-to-side-end distance (28.1 m), which is half the length. **Do not use 28 m as the depth.**

### Dome height (22.60 m against 24 m)

The figures and where they come from:

- **22.60 m** comes from a single 1887 newspaper description in *El Globo*, quoted in full in [5]: "…coronadas por una inmensa cúpula, cuya altura alcanza 22,60 metros…; …en las naves laterales, de 14,61 de elevación…". Every modern 22.60 m and 14.61 m figure, including [1][2][6][15][17], derives from this one text.
- **24 m** comes from the building's owner, the Museo Reina Sofía [4], and from the City of Madrid's heritage record [3]. Mirador Madrid also gives 24 m. IFEMA gives "22 m".
- **"Almost 25 m"**: in 2026 the restoration scaffold "reaches the dome at almost 25 m" [12][13].

The front elevation, scaled to the measured 56 m length, gives:

| Point | Height above ground |
|---|---|
| Nave crown | 16.0 m |
| Dome shell crown | 24.5 m |
| Lantern / pyramid top | 26.3 m |
| Finial tip | 29.5 m |

The interior floor is about 1.4 m above the exterior ground. This is an estimate from the portico steps (at least 8 risers) and from visitors' heads just showing above the glazing sill.

Measuring from the floor instead of the ground removes most of the conflict:

- 14.61 m + 1.4 m = 16.0 m, which matches the nave crown exactly.
- 22.60 m + 1.4 m = 24.0 m, which matches the 24 m figure.

**Recommendation:**

- Model the dome shell crown at **≈ 24 m above ground** (≈ 22.6 m above the floor).
- Put the zinc pyramid and finial above that, to ≈ 29 m.

This datum explanation is my inference. No source states it.

## Plan

### Drawing and provenance

- Plan drawing: `https://es.wikiarquitectura.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Palacio_Cristal_Madrid_planta.jpg` (541 × 425 px, no scale).
- It carries hand annotations and circles a rear annex marked "ELIMIN…" for removal. The 1975 restoration removed a brick store attached at the back [12, search summary; 4], so this is very likely the **1975 restoration (Hernández-Rubio) survey plan**.
- Scale used here: 0.118 m/px. This makes the wall line 54 m, and the plan's outer line then agrees with the 56.3 m measured at the roof edge.

### Overall form

- Three arms on a crossing: two side arms on the long axis and one back arm. The front arm is replaced by the portico.
- The shape is "planta de cruz griega… de la que resta uno de sus brazos" [4], or "cabecera de iglesia gótica, con ábside, dos brazos y un deambulatorio" [3].
- El Globo describes "tres naves sobre una traza de forma de trébol" [5].

### Crossing

- The crossing is a square of nave width (≈ 9.9 m) with chamfered corners. The nave column lines meet it on 45° chamfers, so in plan the crossing is an irregular octagon.
- There are no columns inside the crossing. The fisheye photo looking straight up shows the dome on a square ring with triangular corner panels [19: "vista superior 8mm"].
- The plan draws a small square of ≈ 4.6 × 4.8 m at the centre. This is probably El Globo's "magno pilón destinado á las plantas acuáticas" [5] or the oculus projection. **Unresolved.**

### Side arms

All values are estimated from the plan, unless marked Catastro.

- Width between outer aisle walls: ≈ 17.8–18.4 m.
- Nave between column lines: ≈ 9.8–10.0 m.
- Aisles: ≈ 3.9–4.2 m each.
- Straight run of the aisle wall, from the re-entrant corner or portico pavilion to where the end polygon starts: ≈ 9.8 m at the eaves (Catastro). That is **4 bays**.
- The outer aisle wall closes in **6 facets**: half of a regular 12-gon, 30° per facet.
  - Circumradius ≈ 9.2 m.
  - Facet length ≈ 4.7 m, which is 2 bays per facet.
  - There is a vertex on the arm axis.
  - The polygon centre is ≈ 18.4 m from the crossing centre (Catastro arc centre 18.3 m).
- The nave ends in **3 facets**: half a hexagon, 60° per facet, with a flat end facet.
  - The chamfers start ≈ 17.1 m from the crossing centre.
  - The end facet is ≈ 21.7 m from the crossing centre.
  - In the plan, the end facet looks somewhat shorter than the two diagonals (≈ 3.8 m against ≈ 5.4 m). At this resolution (1 px = 0.12 m, lines 2–3 px wide) I treat that as drawing or measurement noise. The 3-equal-facet model is kept.
- The nave-end and aisle-end polygons are roughly concentric: centres within about 1 m.
- Dashed hip lines in the plan run from the nave-end vertices to the outer-wall vertices. Short stub walls (aisle ribs) also tie the nave corners to the outer wall.
- Each arm gets about 4 + 12 + 4 = **20 perimeter bays** (estimated).

### Back arm

- From the crossing centre: wall ≈ 22.5 m (plan); roof edge 24.1 m (Catastro, OSM).
- Its straight sides are short: ≈ 6 m, about 2–3 bays.
- Its end polygon is similar to the side arms'. Its nave end is ≈ 17.6 m from the crossing centre (plan).
- The plan shows hatched parallel lines (steps?) in the aisle at the back end. **Unresolved:** it could be a stair to a basement or service area.
- **Conflict:** Catastro and OSM give a back-arm width of 17.0 m at the eaves, against 19.6–19.9 m for the side arms. The orthophoto gives ≈ 18.4 m. The plan draws the back arm as wide as, or slightly wider than, the side arms (18.3 m against 17.8 m at the walls). The total widths in question are 17–18.4 m, so a modeller can reasonably use the same nave and aisle section for all three arms.

### Front

- The front wall of the side-arm aisles is ≈ 9.9 m in front of the crossing centre (Catastro).
- The portico block is ≈ 17.2 m wide (Catastro and elevation; the plan draws it ≈ 22 m including the side pavilions) and projects ≈ 4 m.
- The lunette bay behind the portico (the stub of the fourth arm) has nave width.

### Column count

- Perimeter: about 20 bays per side arm and about 16 for the back arm, plus the front stretches. That makes **≈ 60–65 perimeter columns** (estimate).
- Interior nave columns: one per perimeter bay along the straight nave sides, one at each nave-end vertex, plus the crossing chamfer columns. Seen in [19: 2019-09-04 03].

### How the nave is raised

- Tall cast-iron nave columns carry the nave walls.
- Low longitudinal semicircular arches between the nave columns tie the aisle.
- Curved aisle ribs act as flying buttresses: they span from the outer wall columns to the nave columns.
- The UCM text [5] says Velázquez took from Baltard's Les Halles "la elevación de la nave central sobre las laterales, recurriendo a unos arcos que nos recuerdan… las catedrales góticas". WikiArquitectura calls the section basilical, with the aisles acting as buttresses [1].
- Interior photos: [19: interior 08; 2019-09-04 03]; 1887 photo: wikiarquitectura `_25.jpg`.

## Nave and aisle roofs

### Nave vault

- A glazed barrel vault, called "bóvedas de (medio) cañón" in [3][4].
- From the elevation, the vault rises only ≈ 2.9–3 m above the clerestory cornice over a ≈ 9.9 m span. That is a **segmental or elliptical profile with rise ≈ 0.3 of the span** (segment radius ≈ 5.5 m).
  - Interior photos looking along a nave suggest a slightly higher rise, ≈ 0.35 of the span.
  - A semicircular nave vault, with a 4.95 m rise, is **not** supported by the elevation. (estimated)
- At the nave end, the vault turns into three hipped facets over the 3-facet apse. They converge on the last transverse rib [19: 2019-09-04 03].
- Ribs are spaced at the bay (≈ 2.3–2.45 m), with lighter glazing bars between them.

### Aisle roof

- A convex curved lean-to, steeper at the eave.
- It rises ≈ 3 m from the eave (≈ 7.5 m) to the clerestory sill (≈ 10.6 m) over ≈ 4.3–5 m horizontal, including the overhang.
- Model it as a quarter-ellipse with semi-axes of about 4.5 m horizontal and 3 m vertical. (estimated)
- Round the arm ends, the roof is faceted to match the 6-facet wall, with hips on the radial lines in the plan.

### Clerestory

- Vertical glazed wall, ≈ 2.4 m tall.
- **Semicircular-headed windows, one per bay** along the nave sides, and **two per end facet** (photo [19: 2019-09-04 03]). The elevation shows 8 arches along the visible side, at ≈ 2.3 m centres.
- Each window is divided by mullions into narrower lights, with an inner arch.

## Dome

### Type and base

- A four-panel glazed cloister vault ("cúpula esquifada y acristalada de cuatro paños") [1][2]. EcuRed adds "cuatro paños curvos".
- It stands on a square drum over the crossing. The drum base is ≈ 9.9–10.1 m wide in the elevation.
- The drum has a glazed band of round-arched windows. Above it is a frieze of diagonal (X) lattice in triangular panels, visible in exterior photos [19: exterior 01, 20, 22].
- The drum rises above the nave roofs: its lattice band is at ≈ 16.4–17.7 m.

### Profile

- In the elevation, the shell rises ≈ 6.7 m above a ≈ 5 m half-width. **The profile is pointed (ogival), not semicircular.**
- A two-centred pointed arch with radius ≈ 7.0 m fits both the crown and the measured width 2.8 m up (8.8 m).
- Each arc's centre is on the springing line, ≈ 2 m beyond the axis on the opposite side.
- Measured from the photos, the outline reads as a bell or pointed curve. (estimated)

### Top

- The four panels end at a small square opening (≈ 1/5 of the base, ≈ 2 m) with an X brace, seen from inside [19: vista superior 8mm].
- Above the opening sits a **zinc pyramid** ("gran pieza de zinc con forma piramidal") [12]. On it is a finial with a weathervane.
- The elevation drawing shows a small lantern ring under the pyramid at ≈ 24.5–25 m.

### Front gable

- On the front (portico) face, a low gable frames the lunette. Its apex is ≈ 17.1 m.
- The gable slope is ≈ 29°: a 2.75 m rise over a 5 m half-width (elevation). (estimated)

## Windows and glazing

### Perimeter (aisle) bays

- Each bay is a semicircular-headed opening between cast-iron columns.
- The arch has a ceramic archivolt: polychrome star and rosette tiles, of Zuloaga type.
- The opening is ≈ 2.3 m wide and runs from the sill at ≈ 2.1–2.9 m to the crown at ≈ 6.9–7.4 m. That is a width-to-height ratio of about 1 : 1.9 (photo ratio).
- Arch radius ≈ half the bay, ≈ 1.15 m, springing from the impost blocks at ≈ 5.5–6.1 m. (estimated)
- Glazing pattern within each bay ([19: exterior 15, 22]):
  - 3 vertical mullions make 4 lights of ≈ 0.55 m.
  - The two middle mullions carry an inner semicircular arch.
  - A transom runs at impost level.
  - There are few horizontal bars.

### Panes

- The 1990s restoration used large rigid (curved) sheets. These caused condensation and breakage.
- The 2024–27 works replace them with "cristales cuadrados de seguridad… colocados solapados" (overlapping square panes) and add ventilation windows [12][13].
- More than 5,000 panes in total [12][13].
- No pane size is published. **Unresolved.**

### Front lunette

- A semicircular arch over the portico terrace, on short jambs.
- Outer archivolt ≈ 9.9 m wide (radius ≈ 4.9 m); glazed light ≈ 9.2 m wide.
- Crown at ≈ 16.3 m (outer) / 15.6 m (glass).
- Subdivision: about 9 vertical mullions, plus an inner concentric ring with radial bars ([19: exterior 21]; elevation). (estimated)

## Iron structure

- **Material:** iron throughout, except the portico [3]. WikiArquitectura says wrought iron, with cast-iron ornament [1]. The iron was cast in Bilbao [7].
- **Foundry:** the column bases carry "CONSTRUCTOR B. ASINS EN HIERRO – Chamartín 28 Madrid" and "ALONSO MILLAN Y Cª" (Aurrera works, Sestao) [18]. This is a single source (blog), consistent with [7].
- **Perimeter columns:** slender, fluted shafts with Ionic-derived capitals and Greek-key impost blocks, standing on stone pedestal blocks within the ceramic dado.
  - Shaft ≈ 0.15–0.20 m diameter.
  - ≈ 3.3–3.5 m from dado to impost. (estimated)
  - El Globo: "jónicas columnas de hierro" [5].
- **Interior nave columns:** tall cast-iron columns with intermediate rings where the arcade arches and aisle ribs spring.
  - They run from the floor to the clerestory cornice, ≈ 11.5 m above the floor.
  - Diameter ≈ 0.25 m. (estimated from photos)
- **Arches:**
  - Perimeter arches: semicircular, with a decorative ceramic face.
  - Nave arcade: low semicircular arches between nave columns.
  - Aisle ribs: curved quarter arches spanning outer column to nave column.
  - Nave and dome ribs: curved, at bay spacing.
  - Most members appear to be built-up solid or open-web flat sections. True lattice girders are visible only in the drum frieze. There is no measured section data. **Unresolved.**
- **Tie rods:** not documented. Light tie bars are visible in the X-braced oculus.
- **Gutters:** cast-iron angel or mascaron heads at every perimeter column on the aisle cornice. They support the gutters and act as spouts [1][15]. There is one head per bay.
- **Zinc:** decorative zinc elements and the dome pyramid. Restored in 1994–98 [2][3] and again in 2024–27 [12].

## Base

The base layers, from the bottom (photo [19: exterior 15, 22]):

1. Granite base course, ≈ 0.4–0.5 m.
2. Red brick plinth with ventilation grilles, ≈ 0.8–0.9 m of brick.
3. Moulded limestone sill or cornice, top at ≈ 1.6–1.8 m.
4. A dado of stone pedestals under each column. Between the pedestals are **Zuloaga ceramic panels**, ≈ 0.5 m high: griffins, dragons, ducks' heads and grotesques, with cartouches [1][6][17].

Sources describe the platform as "base de piedra y ladrillo" [4] or "basamento de ladrillo… friso de cerámica" [1][2]. These are consistent.

The interior floor is ≈ 1.4 m above the exterior ground (estimated). All plinth heights are estimates ([M2](#methods-for-the-estimates), [M3](#methods-for-the-estimates)).

## Portico and setting

### Portico

- **Type:** a tetrastyle Ionic portico, "doble pórtico tetrástilo" [17]. It is the building's only masonry element.
  - Columns and half-columns stand on a base, reached by a broad granite stair [3][1].
  - The columns are stone, on a concrete base [1][15].
- **Layout** (photos [19: exterior 09, 14, 21]):
  - Four free-standing fluted Ionic columns in front.
  - A second row of 4 columns or pilasters behind, framing the glazed entrance.
  - Two corner pavilions, each with a round-arched window with a lion-head keystone.
  - The entablature is topped by a balustraded **terrace** ("terraza cuadrada con balaustrada corrida") [3].
- **Dimensions** (estimated from [19: exterior 21] with people as scale):
  - Column ≈ 5 m tall including base and capital, shaft ≈ 0.55–0.65 m in diameter.
  - Centre spacing ≈ 2.0–2.1 m; outer column centres ≈ 6.2 m apart.
  - Entablature ≈ 1.2–1.4 m; balustrade ≈ 0.9 m.
  - Overall ≈ 7–7.5 m above the portico floor.
  - Elevation: terrace parapet top ≈ 9.5 m above ground at the lake side; portico block ≈ 17.2 m wide.

### Stairs and lake terrace

- Steps up to the portico: at least 8 risers, ≈ 1.3–1.5 m.
- In front of the portico, the orthophoto [11] shows a **paved balustraded terrace** ≈ 19 m wide, reaching ≈ 24.5 m from the crossing centre (≈ 15 m in front of the main front wall). Steps lead from it **down into the water**: the "escalera sumergida" [8].
- In 1887 the terrace and its balustrade met the lake directly (wikiarquitectura `_10.jpg`).

### Lake

- **Area:**
  - Official figure 2,900 m², volume 3,480 m³ (Ayuntamiento, via [8]).
  - The OSM polygon is 3,540 m², irregular and rounded, with a bounding box of ≈ 70 × 82 m.
  - The equivalent circular diameter is 61–67 m.
  - "70 m diameter" therefore describes its extent only roughly; it is not a circle.
- **Position:** the lake centroid is ≈ 57 m from the crossing centre, bearing ≈ 068°. Its nearest edge is ≈ 12 m from the portico front (OSM).
- **Plants:** bald cypresses (*Taxodium distichum*) stand in the water [6][8].
- **Fountain:**
  - Today the central jet rises **17 m** [8].
  - "Eleva el agua a 4 m" in [1][3] may refer to the original 1887 fountain, or to the basin under the dome. **Unresolved.**

## History (brief)

- **1886–87:** project and construction. The exhibition ran from 30 June to 30 October 1887 [5].
  - The building was meant to be dismantled and re-erected in Manila [5].
  - It was used afterwards as a store and for fine-arts exhibitions.
- **1975:** restoration by Ángeles Hernández-Rubio. Later additions, including the rear brick store, were removed and the original appearance was recovered [4].
- **1994–98:** restoration by José de la Dehesa Romero [3][4]. MonumentalNet says 1984–98 [2]. It consolidated the ironwork, renewed services, and reinstated the ceramics and zinc. It also installed the large rigid panes.
- **2023:** iron pieces fell and the building closed.
- **2024–27:** emergency works, €7.46 M, director Javier Contreras [12][14]:
  - full restoration of the iron structure and glazed envelope, portico, floor and zinc pyramid
  - more than 5,000 panes replaced by overlapping square safety panes
  - reopening planned for July 2027.

## Open questions and unresolved conflicts

1. **Width "28 m".** Not supported by any measurement. Measured depth: 31.4 m at the walls, 34 m at the eaves, 38 m with the portico.
2. **Dome 22.60 m against 24 m.** Probably interior floor against ground (a 1.4 m difference). This is an inference, not stated anywhere. The finial adds about 5 m.
3. **Back-arm width.** Catastro and OSM give 17.0 m, the orthophoto 18.4 m, the plan as wide as the side arms.
4. **Nave and aisle split.** The plan gives a nave of ≈ 9.8–10.0 m and an aisle of ≈ 3.9–4.4 m. Your working values are nave ~9.5 m and aisle ~4.6 m: the nave is within the error, but **the aisle looks about 0.4–0.6 m narrower** in the plan. Both give the same total arm width (≈ 18.4–18.7 m).
5. **Nave-end facets.** The plan reads the end facet ≈ 3.8 m and the diagonals ≈ 5.4 m. This is kept as 3 equal facets of 60°, per your top-view check.
6. **Nave vault profile.** The elevation shows a segmental vault with rise ≈ 0.3 of the span, not a semicircle. This should be checked against an oblique aerial or interior survey.
7. **"Built area 750 m²"** against the 1,290 m² footprint. No explanation found.
8. **Iron sections, pane sizes, rib spacing in detail.** No published survey was found. Restoration documents (Dehesa 1994; Contreras 2024) are not online.
9. **Plan provenance.** It appears to be the 1975 survey (it marks the rear annex for removal). The elevation's provenance is unknown.

## Methods for the estimates

- **M1, plan:** pixel measurements on the 541 × 425 px plan. Dark-line runs were read along the arm axes and across the arms with Python/PIL, and checked on a 5× enlargement.
  - Scale 0.118 m/px: the wall line 458 px = 54 m. The outer line, 476 px, then gives 56.2 m, which matches Catastro.
  - Uncertainty ≈ ±2–3 px, ≈ ±0.3 m, plus any drafting error.
- **M2, elevation:** pixel measurements on the 400 × 242 px elevation.
  - Scale 0.154 m/px from the overall drawn width (365 px) = 56.3 m at the eaves (Catastro).
  - The same scale makes the nave crown 16.0 m: El Globo's 14.61 m plus the 1.4 m floor. This gives an independent check.
  - Uncertainty ≈ ±1 px, ≈ ±0.15 m per reading, plus drawing accuracy, which is unknown but probably ±5%.
- **M3, photo:** [19: exterior 22], 1920 px wide.
  - The walkers' eye level was taken as the horizon, at an assumed camera height of 1.6 m. That gives 81 px/m at the nearest wall column.
  - Upward camera tilt and the camera-height assumption give about ±15%.
  - Visitors' heads inside showing just above the glazing sill confirm the floor level of ≈ 1.4 m.
- **M4, footprint:**
  - Catastro INSPIRE Buildings WFS polygon, building 2043558VK4724C [9].
  - OSM way 23596451 [10].
  - Both rotated to the building axis (104.1° in UTM).
  - Cross-checked on the PNOA orthophoto [11] at 0.08–0.10 m/px: building edges read to about ±0.5 m. Tall parts lean in the orthophoto (the dome is displaced), so only eave edges were used.
